parked: why do A and B not mix at random? L28.3 is new. It answers that question
three ways and compares them:
- Two extremes: random mixture energy eps per site and entropy kB ln 2; mixing
  wins above kBT = eps/ln 2 = 1.443 eps. New arithmetic.
- Regular solution: Omega = 4 eps (four neighbours, random pairs, 2c(1-c)
  unlike fraction, two bonds per site). Its derivative over kBT is exactly the
  A2 Problem 2 function F(x), with H = Omega/kBT. A2 answer key: x_beta =
  0.85532 at H = 2.5; the critical point is H = 2, kBT = 2 eps. H = 2.5 is
  kBT = 1.6 eps. Spinodal at H = 2.5: c = 0.276 and 0.724. Values checked.
- Exact square lattice (Ising equivalence): kTc/J = 2/ln(1+sqrt 2) = 2.269 with
  J = eps/2 for the lattice gas mapping, so kTc = 1.135 eps. Onsager (1944),
  Phys. Rev. 65, 117, checked with Crossref, added as onsager1944crystal.
  VERIFY the J = eps/2 mapping statement in a textbook before the full lecture.
- L28.6 now uses the A2 regular solution as the phase-field bulk free energy,
  replacing the double-well polynomial. g values at H = 2.5: -0.0681 kBT at
  c = 0.5 and -0.0730 kBT at c = 0.4; g'(0.4) = +0.0945 kBT. The phase-field
  section states that g, kappa, and the mobility are fitted inputs and that
  the regular solution overestimates the separation temperature.
- Cu atom density: 8.5e10 atoms per cubic micrometre, from 8.96 g/cm3 and
  63.546 g/mol. New arithmetic.
