S06: revised to the instructor's simpler seminar scope
=====================================================

Q1 and Q2 remain hand Gaussian elimination, including partial pivoting in Q2.
Q3 asks why L13 introduced several solvers. Supply the common diffusion matrix
without deriving a PDE. For the same A and many b vectors, compare:
- fresh dense elimination each time: m[(2/3)n³ + 2n²];
- reusable dense LU: (2/3)n³ + 2mn²;
- reusable dense Cholesky: (1/3)n³ + 2mn².
For n=1000, m=10: 6.6867e9, 6.8667e8, 3.5333e8 operations.
Then discuss the narrow band and the combination of band storage with Cholesky.
These are arithmetic estimates, not a benchmark of four implementations. The
factor-of-two dense comparison must not be claimed for tridiagonal banded LU
versus banded Cholesky: both have linear scaling here.

Removed from the student-facing explanation: PDE/flux derivation, proof of
positive definiteness, Cholesky recurrence, sealed-boundary discussion, flux
balance exercise, conditioning/reference-error investigation and timing tables.
The matrix is supplied as symmetric positive definite for fixed face concentrations.
The demo now emphasizes operation estimates and the two visible factor/solve calls.
Its supplied physical model and reference checks remain internal to validation.

Assumed 50-minute seminar after L13: 5-minute recap, 10-minute Q1, 15-minute Q2,
15-minute Q3 operation counts and short demo, 5-minute discussion.
Evidence: correct augmented row operations; pivot choices; three operation
estimates; explain separately reuse, bandwidth and symmetry. Exit: what changes
when b changes, and when A changes? Omit PDE derivations until the PDE unit.
Reflect: did students attribute LU savings to one solve rather than reuse? Did
they distinguish reduced dense arithmetic from avoiding zeros through band storage?

Default n=1000; optional million-node setting behind a submit button. Roughly
150 MB working arrays plus browser overhead at maximum size. Plot uses <=1000
points. No large dense matrix is created. Hand answers are in collapsed callouts.
