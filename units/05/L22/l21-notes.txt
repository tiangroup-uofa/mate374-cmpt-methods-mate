Corrections and interpretations of the dictation
------------------------------------------------
- "dy^2/dx^2 + b dy/dx + dx = 0" is read as a y'' + b y' + c y = 0.
- Linearity: the dictation says a linear ODE is one where "all the functions
  only depend on one dependent variable". The page uses the standard
  definition (unknown and derivatives to the first power, coefficients depend
  only on the independent variable).
- Stefan-Boltzmann cooling has no explicit T(t). It is separable, so t(T)
  exists through logarithms and arctangents. The page says so instead of
  saying "no analytical solution".
- Euler error: the dictation gives O(dx). The page gives O(h^2) per step and
  O(h) globally, matching the forward difference of L17.
- IVP/BVP: the dictation says first-order ODEs are usually IVPs and
  second-order ODEs usually BVPs, with no hard rule. The page says the type
  follows from where the conditions are given, and that time problems are IVPs.
- Implicit methods: the page does not say they have no step limit. It states
  the backward-Euler result for y' = -lambda y only, and that both methods are
  O(h).
- "Central method" for better estimates is mentioned in one sentence and left
  to the following lectures.

Cooling problem (all values checked numerically)
------------------------------------------------
- Steel sphere, R = 1 mm, emissivity 0.8, rho = 7800 kg/m^3, c = 500 J/(kg K),
  T0 = 1200 K, T_ambient = 300 K, vacuum, uniform temperature.
- alpha = 3 eps sigma / (rho c R) = 3.49e-11 K^-3 s^-1; initial rate -72.07 K/s.
- Reference: solve_ivp, Radau, rtol 1e-11. T(5 s) = 969.1 K, T(10) = 852.2,
  T(20) = 723.6, T(40) = 599.7, T(60) = 534.1.
- Explicit Euler error at 20 s: h = 5, 2, 1, 0.5 s give -64.7, -20.7, -9.9,
  -4.8 K. h = 20 s gives -241 K at the first step (negative temperature).
- Implicit Euler (Newton on each step), h = 20 s: 846.8, 692.2, 604.6 K at
  20, 40, 60 s. At h = 1 s the error at 20 s is +9.2 K.
- Constant specific heat and emissivity, no convection or conduction inside
  the sphere. These are stated in the lecture.
